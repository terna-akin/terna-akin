AI GOVERNANCE · FICTIONAL FINTECH CASE STUDY

What Happens When a Fintech Gives AI Access to Customer Data?

By Terna Akin-Akinbisola · Adapted from my SentraPay article

AI can help financial institutions answer customers faster and assess loan applications. But convenience alone does not make a system safe.

I developed an AI risk register for SentraPay Microfinance Technologies Limited, a fictional Nigerian fintech. The assessment considered SentraAssist, an AI support chatbot, and SentraScore, an automated credit scoring system.

The question goes beyond whether the model works. Who can access the data? Who approves an action? What happens when an answer is wrong? Using the OWASP LLM Top 10 and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, the case study examined nine areas of risk.

1. Hidden instructions

An AI assistant processing customer messages and internal records must separate retrieved content from trusted instructions. Limit data access and check sensitive outputs.

2. Confident but incorrect guidance

Answers about fees, credit or repayment can affect real decisions. Use approved current information, human review for consequential advice and an obvious route to support.

3. Customer data exposure

A conversation is not proof of identity or permission. Check authorisation for every retrieval and keep sensitive fields masked by default.

4. Unsafe operational actions

Before an assistant changes records or requests a refund, the receiving system should independently validate identity, permissions and the requested action.

5. Unfair credit decisions

Historical patterns can reproduce unfair outcomes. Test across relevant customer groups, review proxy variables and offer human reconsideration.

6. Third-party exposure

Maintain an inventory of providers and integrations, assign owners, restrict access and review dormant connections.

7. Too much autonomy

Keep tool permissions narrow. Consequential actions need independent approval, action limits, audit logs and a reliable shutdown process.

8. Model probing

Repeated applications may help an attacker infer a scoring model’s behaviour. Limit repeated queries and monitor coordinated activity.

9. AI-enabled impersonation

A familiar voice or face is insufficient verification for a financial request. Confirm consequential actions through an independent channel already held on file.

Governance before scale

Safe deployment, independent authorisation and measurable accountability should be conditions for growth. Give each system a named owner, test its behaviour, review access and keep high-impact decisions subject to meaningful human oversight.

For fintechs, prevention must begin before the system reaches the customer.

SentraPay is a fictional learning case study. This article presents its risk assessment and proposed controls, rather than results from a client deployment.
